## After pre-registration (tag `v1.0-prereg`, frozen 2026-11-07)

- **One task published on its own (2026-11-19).** The frozen method said that if results were published, every pre-registered task would be shown, not a selection. This repository publishes one of the seven.
- **No vendor review (2026-11-19).** The frozen method said each competitor would be offered its own results and the method before publication. That was not done.
- **Intervals added (2026-11-19).** The published scores now carry 95% intervals. They were added after the results were known and are not part of the frozen method (METHODOLOGY section 7).
- **Harbor setup check (2026-11-19).** One run of write-precall-briefs in a Harbor container, with API-key-only Clay access (see the first entry under "This repository"). Logged in REPRODUCTIONS.md. Graders leave metrics they couldn't compute out of reward.json (Harbor rejects null), and the reviewer falls back to the signed-in Claude CLI outside the container.
- **Task file wording (2026-11-19, editorial).** "(proposed)" removed from the frozen quality floor, the input path points at `private/datasets/`, and the competitors' platform notes say their tier names and product notes are not published. The frozen text stays at the tag.
- **Harbor task (2026-11-18).** The task was added as a Harbor task, with a grader applying the scorer's rules; its brief counts reproduce our runs. A live Harbor run showed the API key alone can't reach what our CLI-signed-in runs used, so the task takes the workspace's saved routine IDs (`harbor/clay-routine-env.sh`) and its instruction ends with one environment note. The original prompt in `config/bench.json` is unchanged. The reviewer header, model and rubric have one source (the Harbor task's `judge.py`, and `scripts/ai_review.py`), with the review prompt unchanged byte for byte.
- **Provider A price (2026-11-10, after the runs).** Provider A's price was filled in privately, at its self-serve top-up rate (the marginal add-on rate, not a negotiated or plan-included rate).
- **Scoring and review fixes (2026-11-10, after the freeze and after the first results had been seen):**
  - **Blind sample, same accounts (benchkit).** The review covers the same 20 accounts on every platform, as the spec says; the sampler had drawn separately per run (1 of 20 shared). Now, when every run covers the same domains, one shared set is sampled.
  - **Review method (was: a person and an AI both rating every sampled brief).** An AI reviewer (Claude Sonnet 5.5 with web search; the agents ran on Opus 5.5) checked every sampled brief with the same rubric, and the person audited fresh random 20% samples blind. Agreement was 58% in round 1 and 78% in round 2, after clarifying the rules; both are under the 85% bar set beforehand. Rules clarified between rounds: a criterion that can't be confirmed from public sources is not met; a fact is fabricated only if shown false or not found after searching press releases, wire services and SEC filings; usefulness ignores whether the account is a typical SaaS buyer. The task spec's wording for a fabrication was "a fact that's false or can't be checked". The AI reviewed every brief again under the clarified rules; METHODOLOGY section 7 shows the scores under both passes. Final scores: the AI's second-pass ratings plus the person's round-2 ratings as a second rater.
